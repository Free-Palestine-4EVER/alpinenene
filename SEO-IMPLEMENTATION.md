# Alpine Sauber search visibility

This implementation adds crawlable service URLs and machine-readable business information without changing the site’s visual design.

## Added to the site build

- A German-Austrian title, description, canonical URL, social preview metadata, and structured data for each page.
- Static pages for all 12 services, plus Leistungen, Über uns, Kontakt, Impressum, and Datenschutz. Service cards keep their current detail-sheet design and now have real URLs that can be shared, crawled, and opened directly.
- `robots.txt` with access for search crawlers and named AI search crawlers.
- `sitemap.xml` generated from the canonical page list.
- `llms.txt` and `llms-full.txt` with business facts and service summaries.
- LocalBusiness, WebSite, WebPage, Service, and breadcrumb JSON-LD using the business address, phone, and opening hours already published by Alpine Sauber.

The LLM crawler rules allow search and user-request crawlers. They do not add a training opt-out: the existing catch-all rule allowed unspecified crawlers, including training crawlers, and this change preserves that policy. If Alpine Sauber wants to opt out of AI training crawlers, that should be a separate explicit policy decision.

`llms.txt` is included because Alpine Sauber wants to support LLM discovery. It is an emerging convention, not a Google ranking mechanism or a guarantee that an AI system will use the file.

## Production handoff

At the time of this implementation, `https://alpinesauber.at` was still serving the WordPress site and its Rank Math sitemap. These files are generated by the Vite project and only become the live domain’s `robots.txt`, sitemap, and page routes if this build is actually deployed to that domain. Do not run WordPress and this build as competing root sites. If WordPress remains the production host, mirror the page metadata, structured data, and crawler files in WordPress instead; preserve its current canonical URLs.

## External steps requiring the owner’s accounts

1. In Google Search Console, verify the `alpinesauber.at` Domain property, submit `https://alpinesauber.at/sitemap.xml`, and inspect the home page and all 12 service URLs. Resolve any indexing, canonical, mobile usability, or structured-data issues it reports.
2. In Bing Webmaster Tools, verify the same domain and submit the sitemap. Enable IndexNow if the WordPress/Vercel production setup supports it.
3. In Google Business Profile, keep the public business name, address, phone, hours, categories, service area, and services consistent with the site. Add current original job photos and request honest reviews from customers; do not use review markup for reviews that are not displayed on the site.
4. Keep the website’s phone, address, email, and hours identical across the Business Profile and legitimate local directories. Earn relevant local mentions and links through real customer, supplier, and community relationships.
5. Check Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools monthly. Review impressions, clicks, queries, indexed URLs, crawl errors, and calls or form enquiries. Improve pages based on actual search terms and customer questions rather than publishing near-duplicate city pages.

Before launch, confirm whether the Brandreinigung page’s existing “24/7 Notfallservice” promise is accurate; the site’s general published hours are Monday–Saturday, 08:00–18:00. The schema deliberately uses the general published hours and does not repeat the 24/7 claim.

Google documents that AI features use the same core SEO foundations and that `llms.txt` is not used for Google Search. ChatGPT Search discovery depends on allowing `OAI-SearchBot`; the training crawler is separate. Local rankings also depend on relevance, distance, and prominence, so no technical change can promise a number-one position in Austria or Graz.
